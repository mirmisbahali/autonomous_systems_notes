# Learning principles

Use these principles as guardrails, not as reasons to add more prose.

- **Retrieval before rereading:** active recall strengthens later retention more than passive restudy. Ask the learner to reconstruct an explanation before showing it.
- **Feedback after an attempt:** correct the smallest important gap after retrieval so an error is not rehearsed.
- **Atomic but connected notes:** keep one reusable idea per concept note, then link prerequisites, contrasts, examples, and implementations. Atomicity supports targeted retrieval; links preserve system context.
- **Concrete plus intuitive examples:** include one technically faithful example and one compact analogy or scenario. Do not let the analogy replace the mechanism.
- **Elaboration with restraint:** add why a concept exists, how it works, and a failure mode. Remove facts that do not serve understanding, application, or interview explanation.
- **Evidence-aware mastery:** distinguish being able to recognise or explain an idea from independently implementing, debugging, and integrating it.
- **No false scheduling claim:** this vault uses retrieval prompts and review metadata, but no due-date algorithm. Call it active-recall support, not automated spaced repetition.

Primary references:

- Karpicke, J. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2011), *Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping*, Science. <https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1199327>
- Dunlosky, J. et al. (2013), *Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques*, Psychological Science in the Public Interest. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266>
